(a) O0/O1/O2 classification  (reproduces design Appendix A)
    For each output bit o, D(o) = its input-bit dependency set.
      O0: D(o) subset of S        (computable from the S half alone)
      O1: D(o) subset of S' = ~S  (computable from the S' half alone)
      O2: mixed                   (needs both halves; combined separately)
    A function admits the split-pack / linear-reassembly design iff O0,O1,O2
    partition cleanly; all four do.

    Sigma0: |S|=16  O0=11  O1=11  O2=10
    Sigma1: |S|=16  O0=11  O1=11  O2=10
    sigma0: |S|=16  O0=11  O1=11  O2=10
    sigma1: |S|=16  O0=12  O1=12  O2=8
    Maj,Ch: bit-local, O2=0 — any partition of the word's bits is sound.

    NOTE (this branch): the O0/O1/O2 decode tables and the packed Maj/Ch table
    are NOT committed in the mdoc/shared path. Sigma/sigma/Maj/Ch are evaluated
    from full 32-bit-plane boolean columns (constrain_boolean_bits +
    big_sigma0_expr_bits / maj_expr_bits / ch_expr_bits in constraints.rs).
    The classification above is the *soundness basis* the split-pack tuple
    inherits; it is reproduced here to show the redesign does not disturb it.

(b) Per-limb fragmentation — the redesign EXISTENCE argument
    A packed group value is  packed(G) = sum_k bit_{pos_k(G)} * 2^k  over the
    sorted bit positions of G. This is LINEAR in the bits. A group G that
    straddles a limb boundary splits into per-limb fragments
        G = union_L (G ∩ L)
    and recombines by a single degree-1 identity
        packed(G) = sum_L  packed(G ∩ L) * 2^{offset_L}
    where offset_L = (# bits of G below limb L). Because packing is linear,
    this recombination is ALWAYS available, for every function and every limb
    width — the existence argument is unconditional. The only thing that varies
    with width is the *fragment count* (drives table columns + recombine terms).

    Model: split-pack table keyed by a w-bit limb, outputting the two
    fragments (S ∩ limb) and (S' ∩ limb) for that limb (both <= w bits, so both
    are legal table-value cells with a free range check by construction).

    width | function | #limbs | total nonempty (S,S') frags | max frag bits
    ------+----------+--------+-----------------------------+--------------
       8  | Sigma0   |   4    |            8                |   5
       8  | Sigma1   |   4    |            8                |   6
       8  | sigma0   |   4    |            8                |   5
       8  | sigma1   |   4    |            8                |   5
      12  | Sigma0   |   3    |            6                |   7
      12  | Sigma1   |   3    |            6                |   7
      12  | sigma0   |   3    |            6                |   7
      12  | sigma1   |   3    |            6                |   7
      16  | Sigma0   |   2    |            4                |   9
      16  | Sigma1   |   2    |            4                |  10
      16  | sigma0   |   2    |            4                |   9
      16  | sigma1   |   2    |            4                |   8

    Reading: every limb of every function carries BOTH an S and an S' fragment
    (2 fragments/limb, uniformly). So under w=8 each word-partition needs
    4 limbs * 2 = 8 fragments (vs the current 16-bit scheme's 4 per half = 8,
    identical fragment budget) but each fragment now lives in a 2^8-row table
    instead of a 2^16-row table.

    Degree check: recombination is degree 1; the split-pack LogUp tuple is a
    membership use (numerator = +mult on the consumer, -mult on the producer),
    the producer emits one gated fraction per limb-table (finalize_logup_in_pairs,
    degree <= 2 after lifting). No constraint exceeds degree 2. PAIRS-ONLY
    batching preserved. Bound log_size+1 holds for every producer component.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
(c) Why w=8 and not smaller/larger
    Fragment budget is width-independent (always 2/limb). Table ROWS are 2^w.
    - w<8 (e.g. 4): tables already negligible at w=8; going smaller cannot save
      more table mass but keeps DOUBLING the consumer split-pack lookup count
      (4 nibbles vs 2 bytes per half). Pure loss below w=8.
    - w=8: tables collapse to 2^9 blinded (well above SIMD floor 2^4); consumer
      split-pack lookups exactly double (half -> 2 bytes). Sweet spot.
    - w=12: uneven (12+12+8), needs a Range_12 (2^13) table too; tables 8x
      bigger than w=8 while saving only ~0.5x the consumer lookups. Rejected.
    - w=16: status quo (rows stay 2^17); no table-mass win.
